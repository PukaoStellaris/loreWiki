{astral}Astral{/} wandered around the small, damp room, her fingers nervously drumming against the hilt of her katana. It wasn't just any blade; it was **"Falling Up,"** a weapon that hummed with a faint, violet resonance, a permanent gravity enchantment placed on it by her best friend.

When the burst of energy shook from the entrance, Astral didn't glare. She let out a breath she didn't realize she'd been holding.

"You're late, Sen," Astral said, her voice filled with relief rather than scolding. She moved to help the stumbling figure. "I was about five seconds away from blowing this whole tunnel open to find you."

"And ruin the secret meeting spot? The Council would have a field day," {sentinel}Sentinel{/} grinned, leaning heavily against the table. She brushed ash off her cloak. "Sorry, Astral. Those 'Doctors' are persistent. They really don't like it when you borrow from the restricted section."

"You mean steal," Astral corrected, handing her a water container.

"Borrowing with style," Sentinel winked, taking a long drink. She glanced at the sword on Astral's hip. "You're still carrying 'Falling Up'? I thought the Imperial Aegis issued standard rapiers to their Majors."

"They do," Astral rested her hand on the gravity-imbued hilt. "But standard rapiers don't cut through heavy armor. Without your gravity enchantment on this, I'm just fast. I needed the weight you gave me."

Sentinel's expression softened. The playful attitude vanished, replaced by a nostalgic warmth.

"That's why I'm here, Astral," Sentinel whispered. "Remember back at **Sovereign Saints**? Second year?"

Astral laughed softly. "How could I forget? You were failing there. You had all that mana but couldn't control the elements. They were going to expel you."

"Until you broke into the Headmaster's vault," Sentinel pointed out. "You risked your entire future to steal that **Ancient Gravity Scroll** for me. You told me, 'If you can't keep up with the elements, force them to bow to you.'"

Sentinel reached into her ruined cloak and pulled out the brittle, dark parchment she had been guarding.

"You gave me a future, Astral. You made me an Archmage. But I watch you fight... and I see you struggling against the big machines. Your wind is fast, but it lacks... crunch."

She slid the scroll across the table.

"So, I'm returning the favor. I stole this from the deepest quarantine vault the Doctors have."

Astral looked at the scroll. It was vibrating. The wood of the table cracked beneath it. "What is it?"

"**Ancient Shatter Magic**," Sentinel said, her eyes sparkling. "It's not about cutting. It's about grabbing the vibrations in the air and cracking them. Like that old pirate story we used to read? The one who could tilt the seas?"

Astral's eyes widened. "Edward?"

"Exactly. You give them the wind, Astral. But when the wind isn't enough..." Sentinel mimicked a cracking motion with her fist. "...you break the world."

*CREAK.*

The sound wasn't an explosion, but the sickening sound of the heavy iron door being forced open by magic.

"Subject identified," a hollow, distorted voice spoke from the shadows.

Three figures glided into the room. They were humanoid, but their movements were unnervingly fluid and silent. They wore long, tattered black leather coats and wide-brimmed hats. Their faces were entirely hidden behind long, porcelain **plague doctor masks**, the beaks sharp and menacing.

"Major Anemos. Archmage Sentinel," the lead Doctor spoke, tilting its beak. It raised a gloved hand, and green medicinal mana flared around its fingers like a corrosive mist. "The cure is administered through termination."

Sentinel stumbled back, exhausted. "I'm tapped out, Astral. My mana is drained."

Astral drew "Falling Up", but she hesitated. These weren't mindless beasts. They were elite mage-hunters. Her wind blades might be deflected by their corrosive barriers, and she couldn't risk Sentinel getting caught in a gravity crush.

She looked at the scroll. She could feel the vibrations syncing with her heartbeat.

*Use it,* the magic whispered.

"Get behind me, Sen," Astral said, sliding the sword back into its sheath. She grabbed the scroll, the energy rushing into her arm like a flood.

"Astral? What are you doing?" Sentinel asked.

Astral didn't answer. She didn't chant a spell. She simply turned to the approaching figures, clenched her fist, and grabbed the empty air as if it were a solid handle.

**"Shatter Art: Fracture."**

She punched the air.

*CRACK.*

It sounded like the sky itself had snapped. Spiderweb-like fractures of white light appeared in the empty space where her fist connected.

The shockwave was instantaneous. The air didn't just push the Doctors back; it *shattered*. The invisible barrier of the lead Doctor shattered like glass, and the impact launched all three humanoids backward as if they had been hit by a wrecking ball. Their leather coats whipped violently, and the porcelain beak of the leader's mask cracked down the middle before they were blasted out of the tunnel entirely.

Dust rained down on them. Astral stared at her fist, shaking from the recoil.

Sentinel let out a low whistle, her bunny ears twitching. "Okay... we are definitely even."

Astral turned back, a wild, exhilarated grin breaking her usual composure. She reached out and pulled Sentinel into a one-armed hug.

"Not yet," Astral laughed. "Now you have to teach me how to control it before I break my own arm."

> Six Months Later, At the Sovereign Saints Academy (Abandoned Training Hall)

The air in the old training hall smelled of broken physics.

Astral stood in the center of the room, her right arm trembling uncontrollably. Her sleeve was shredded, revealing bruises that looked like spiderwebs running up her skin.

"You're doing it again," Sentinel called out from the arena seats, munching on an apple. "You're trying to *push* the vibration. You have to let it flow through you, or you're going to shatter your arms."

"Easy for you to say," Astral gritted out, gripping **Falling Up**. The gravity enchantment on the katana hummed, grounding her. "Gravity pulls. Shatter... it rejects everything. It's like trying to hold an explosion in a jar."

"So don't be the jar. Be the cannon." Sentinel hopped down, wiping apple juice on her Archmage robes. "Use the sword. I put that gravity charm on it to give you weight, right? Use the gravity to anchor your body, *then* throw the punch."

Astral closed her eyes. She drew the katana, planting the tip into the ground. A violet ring of gravity pulsed out, locking her boots to the floorstone. She took a deep breath, channeling the wind around her left side and the terrifying, cracking vibration of the scroll into her right fist.

She punched empty air.

*CRACK.*

This time, there was no recoil. The air in front of her fractured like a mirror, and the shockwave traveled fifty meters, turning a row of stone training dummies into dust.

Sentinel whistled. "Okay. Now *that* was scary."

Astral looked at her fist. No pain. Only power. "I think I'm ready."

---

> Three Years Later, At the Northern Gate of the Royal Capital

The alarm bells had been ringing for hours. The "Doctors", the rogue faction of magitech enforcers, had unleashed their masterpiece: **The Titan Series**.

Three massive, mechanical giants, towering over the city walls, were marching toward the palace. They were plated in anti-magic gold, rendering standard elemental attacks useless. Fireballs splashed off them; lightning grounded harmlessly.

The current Grand Marshal, an elderly earth mage, lay unconscious near the gate, his defenses crumbled. The Imperial Aegis line was breaking.

"Major!" A lieutenant screamed, ducking under debris. "We can't scratch them! General {xi}Xi{/} is trying to hold the left side, but the center is collapsing!"

Astral walked through the panicked soldiers. She wasn't a Major anymore; she had climbed to **Lieutenant General**, but the rank didn't matter right now. She wore a long black coat over her uniform, the sleeves loose to hide the tremors that still occasionally plagued her arms.

She tapped her earpiece. "Sen? You in position?"

"Way ahead of you," Sentinel's voice crackled, sounding bored. She was sitting on a rooftop three blocks away. "I've got a gravity field prepped under the middle Giant. I can't crush it, it's too dense. but I can trip it."

"Good enough," Astral said. "Drop the shield."

"On it."

On the rooftop, Sentinel raised a hand. **"Gravity Art: Downward Spiral."**

The center Giant stumbled. The gravity around its feet intensified instantly, cracking the pavement and sinking the machine down to its knees. It roared, a sound of grinding metal, and tried to stabilize.

"Now, Astral!" Sentinel shouted over the comms.

Astral didn't run. She let the wind catch her. She vanished, reappearing instantly in the air directly in front of the kneeling Giant's faceplate.

The Construct raised a massive brass arm to swat her like a fly.

Astral drew Falling Up with her left hand, slashing upward. The gravity enchantment clashed with the Construct's arm, parrying the massive limb with impossible weight.

With her right hand free, she pulled back. The air around her fist turned white, a halo of jagged cracks forming in the atmosphere.

**"Shatter Art: Shattering Heaven and Earth."**

She drove her fist into the air inches from the Giant's face.

*KRA-KOOM.*

The sound deafened the entire battlefield. The atmosphere fractured. The shockwave ignored the anti-magic gold plating entirely, traveling directly through the metal and into the machine's core.

The Giant didn't fall over. It disintegrated. The vibration shattered every gear, every piston, and every mana crystal inside it. The massive machine crumbled into a heap of scrap metal.

Astral landed softly on the wreckage, her coat fluttering. She looked at the remaining two Giants. She cracked her knuckles.

The other two Giants stopped moving. Their pilot processor calculated the threat level, turned around, and began to retreat.

> Two Days Later, At the Audience Chamber

The court was silent. The "Siege of the Iron Giants" was over, but the political shockwaves were just beginning.

King Cenric sat on his throne, his gaze piercing. Beside him stood the recovering former Marshal and the {archon}Archon{/} of the Citadel.

"Astral Anemos," King Cenric's voice echoed. "Step forward."

Astral walked to the center of the room. She felt Sentinel's eyes on her from the balcony where the Divinity Council stood. Sentinel gave her a subtle thumbs-up.

"The reports represent a puzzle," the King said, leaning forward. "You parried a Titan Construct with a sword that weighs as much as a mountain. And then... you struck the air, and the machine fell."

The Archon narrowed his eyes. "An anomaly. Wind magic does not possess such violent force."

Astral didn't flinch. She had rehearsed this with Sentinel. "It is an evolution of my technique, Archon. By compressing air to a microscopic point and releasing it, I create a localized sonic boom."

It was a lie. It was Shatter Magic. But as long as she didn't reveal the scroll, they couldn't prove it.

King Cenric looked at her for a long moment. He didn't care about the mechanics. He cared that the city was standing because of her.

"The **Imperial Aegis** needs a leader who does not break when the walls do," the King declared. He stood up. "The current Marshal has requested retirement. He claims he cannot follow a soldier who fights like a natural disaster."

The King drew the ceremonial sword of the Grand Marshal—a blade signifying supreme command over the Kingdom's military.

"Kneel, Astral Anemos."

She knelt.

"Rise," King Cenric commanded, handing her the blade.

> "**Grand Marshal Anemos**. The Shield of the Kingdom."

> Later at night, In the Citadel Gardens

Astral sat on a stone bench, the heavy, ornate badge of the **Grand Marshal** sitting on her lap like a paperweight.

"Does it come with a pay raise?" Sentinel asked, hopping over the back of the bench to sit beside her.

"A massive one," Astral sighed, picking up the badge. "And about ten thousand times more paperwork. I have to reorganize the Generals. I'm thinking of promoting Xi and {mirai}Mirai{/} closer to the inner circle. They're trustworthy."

"Good. You'll need them," Sentinel stole the badge, inspecting it. "Because now that you're the boss, the Archon is going to be watching you like a hawk. He knows wind doesn't crack armor like that."

"Let him watch," Astral took the badge back, pinning it to her chest. She patted the hilt of Falling Up. "We fooled him once with the 'sonic boom' story. We can keep fooling him."

Astral turned to Sentinel, her expression softening.

"I couldn't have done it without the gravity assist, Sen. That parry... if you hadn't sunk the Giant first, I would have been swatted."

Sentinel shrugged, leaning back and looking at the stars. "That's the deal, right? You take the spotlight, I handle the shadows. You're the Shield, I'm the... whatever I am."

"You're the Ace," Astral corrected. "And now that I run the army, I can finally make sure the 'Doctors' stop hunting you. I'm burying your file, Sentinel. You're officially an untouchable asset of the state."

Sentinel smiled, a genuine, rare smile. "Does that mean I don't have to hide in corners anymore?"

"It means," Astral stood up, her black coat waving, "that we have a lot of work to do. But tonight? Drinks on the Grand Marshal."

Sentinel laughed, linking her arm with Astral's. "Finally. I thought you'd never ask."

> Six Months Later, At Grand Marshal's Office, Imperial Castle

The office of the Grand Marshal was far too clean, far too large, and smelled faintly of lemon polish and stress.

Grand Marshal Astral Anemos sat behind a desk made of exotic wood that probably cost more than her childhood home. Stacks of logistics reports towered on either side of her.

"Sign here. And here. And initial here for the mana crystal import tax," a nervous aide squeaked, sliding another document forward.

Astral sighed, the sound sharp enough to make the aide flinch. She signed with an aggressive flourish. "Is that all, Lieutenant?"

"Y-Yes, Grand Marshal! The Council meeting is in an hour." The aide bowed and rushed out, closing the heavy oak doors.

As soon as the latch clicked, the "dignified" silence was broken.

"You look miserable," a voice said from the ceiling.

Sentinel dropped down from the chandelier, landing silently on the expensive carpet. She was wearing her official Archmage robes, though she had modified them with extra pockets (likely for snacks) and wore her signature tie loose.

"I am not miserable," Astral muttered, leaning back and rubbing her face. "I am... efficient. We have peace, Sentinel. The 'Doctors' have been disbanded. The borders are secure. The King is happy."

"And you're bored," Sentinel countered, hopping onto the edge of the wooden desk. She poked the hilt of "Falling Up," which leaned against Astral's chair. "This sword hasn't tasted gravity or blood in months. It's getting lonely."

Astral cracked a small smile, looking at her friend. "It's better than running, Sen. I looked at your file this morning. 'Archmage Sentinel: Security Protection Level 10.' You're untouchable now. No more hiding in damp corners."

"Yeah, about that," Sentinel picked up a paperweight, tossing it up and catching it with a gravity field. "It's nice not being hunted. But the Citadel is getting restless. The Archon keeps looking at me like I'm a lab rat he hasn't figured out how to dissect yet."

"Let him look," Astral's voice hardened. "He answers to the King. And the King listens to me."

*KNOCK. KNOCK.*

The door opened again, but this time, it wasn't a nervous aide. It was General Xi, looking grim.

"Grand Marshal," Xi saluted sharply, ignoring Sentinel's presence on the desk. "Urgent report from the Southern Ruins. Our scouts have gone dark."

Astral got up instantly, the 'bored office worker' vanishing. "Dark? Was it a monster surge?"

"No, Ma'am," Xi handed over a tablet. "We recovered a single audio transmission before the signal cut. It was a speech."

Astral took the tablet. She played the audio.

*Static... screaming... then a calm, mocking voice.* "...tell the Aegis that the Executioners have returned."

The room went cold.

"Executioners," Sentinel whispered, the playful light leaving her eyes. "That's the old clan. The mage-killers. I thought they were wiped out decades ago."

"Apparently not," Astral said, grabbing her coat from the rack. She strapped "Falling Up" to her hip. The violet rune on the hilt pulsed, sensing her intent. "General Xi, mobilize the elite vanguard. I want the Divinity Council on alert."

"You're going personally, Ma'am?" Xi asked, surprised. "Protocol states—"

"Protocol states that if a threat wipes out my scouts, I wipe out the threat," Astral interrupted, walking toward the balcony doors.

She turned to Sentinel.

"You said you were bored, Archmage. Feel like stretching your legs?"

Sentinel grinned, a spark of dangerous, chaotic mana crackling around her fingers. "I thought you'd never ask, Marshal. I'll bring the gravity."

"And I," Astral clenched her fist, the air around it vibrating with the power of the **Shatter Scroll**, "will bring the impact."

They walked out onto the balcony together, overlooking the kingdom they had sworn to protect. The sun was setting, casting long shadows over the Citadel, foreshadowing the darkness that was yet to come.

The days of hiding were over. The era of the **Imperial Aegis** and the **{violet}Violet Nihility{/}** had begun.
